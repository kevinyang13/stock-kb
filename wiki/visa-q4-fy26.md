# Visa Q4 FY26

**Summary**: Visa's fiscal fourth quarter 2026 (quarter ended September 30, 2026): $12,139M of net revenue, up 13% year over year, a 60% operating margin and $6,002M of net income. Visa reports no cost of revenue at all, so every cost on the chart sits in operating expenses — and the largest single deduction, $5,123M of client incentives, never appears as a cost because it is netted out of revenue before the statement starts.

**Sources**: Visa 8-K exhibit 99.1, filed October 27, 2026 (source: sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001403161/000140316126000126/q42026earningsrelease.htm). Quote data, debt and cash from stockanalysis.com, read 2026-10-27. Street EPS consensus from the Nasdaq/Zacks endpoints, read 2026-10-27.

**Last updated**: 2026-10-27

## The line that is missing: cost of revenue

Visa publishes **no cost of revenue and no gross profit**. The statement runs net revenue →
operating expenses → operating income, with nothing in between. That is not an omission in the
chart: a payment network's cost of running the rails is personnel, marketing, network and
processing, and so on, all of which Visa classifies as operating expenses.

The consequence on the Financials card is a **gross margin of 100%**, which is literal rather than
an error. [[nebius-q2-fy26]] and most other cards show a real cost line; Visa and Nu Holdings do
not, and the chart now suppresses the empty "Cost of revenue" node rather than drawing a $0 label
with no bar under it. See [[sankey-income-statement]].

## Revenue disaggregation, and why the chart draws one bar

Visa reports **one operating segment**. Its revenue disaggregation is by category, and every
category is stated **gross of client incentives**:

| Category | Q4 FY26 | Y/Y |
|---|---|---|
| Service revenue | 5,255 | +14% |
| Data processing revenue | 6,245 | +16% |
| International transaction revenue | 4,115 | +8% |
| Other revenue | 1,647 | +40% |
| Client incentives | (5,123) | +21% |
| **Net revenue** | **12,139** | **+13%** |

The four positive categories sum to **$17,262M** — a total Visa does not report anywhere — and
client incentives are a **deduction from revenue**, not a segment. A sankey cannot draw a negative
band, and drawing the four categories alone would put $17.3B of segments against a $12.1B revenue
bar, which is exactly the sum check every chart on this site has to pass.

So the chart draws a single `Net revenue` bar and the card carries the full table. This is the
first card where a real, published disaggregation could not be drawn as bands. It is **not** the
"company publishes no segment split" case that [[favorites-financials]] records for thirteen other
names.

**Client incentives are the figure to watch.** At $5,123M they are 30% of gross revenue and they
grew 21% year over year, against 13% growth in net revenue — incentives are growing faster than
the revenue they buy. Service revenue is recognised on the *prior* quarter's payments volume;
every other category is recognised on the current quarter's activity.

## Where the quarter came from

Key business drivers, year-over-year change in constant dollars:

| Driver | Q4 FY26 | Full year |
|---|---|---|
| Payments volume | +9% | +9% |
| Cross-border volume (total) | +14% | +13% |
| Cross-border volume ex intra-Europe | +14% | +12% |
| Processed transactions | +10% | +9% |

Processed transactions were 74.2 billion for the quarter. Net revenue grew 13% on 9% payments
volume growth, so price and mix — above all the 16% growth in data processing revenue and the 40%
jump in other revenue — did more work than volume.

## Operating expenses

The chart groups the seven filed expense lines into four bands so the leaf labels stay legible:
personnel, marketing and general and administrative as filed, and `Other operating` ($1,466M)
combining network and processing ($293M), professional fees ($322M), depreciation and amortisation
($377M) and the litigation provision ($474M).

Two lines moved sharply. **General and administrative rose 52%** to $829M, which Visa names as the
main driver of the 7% increase in total GAAP operating expenses. **The litigation provision fell
48%** to $474M; it was $903M a year ago, and had it stayed at last year's level total opex would have grown about
16% rather than 7%. The litigation provision is the US covered litigation escrow, a recurring but
lumpy line.

## GAAP against non-GAAP

| Measure | Q4 FY26 | Y/Y |
|---|---|---|
| GAAP net income | $6.0B | +18% |
| GAAP EPS | $3.19 | +22% |
| Non-GAAP net income | $6.6B | +13% |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $3.48 | +17% |

The income statement chart is **GAAP**. The earnings-surprise dots on the same card are the
street's adjusted figure — $3.48 actual against a $3.43 consensus, a 1.5% beat — which is the
non-GAAP number, not the $3.19 the sankey is built from. That split is the whole reason the
[[earnings-surprise-chart]] note exists.

GAAP EPS grew faster than GAAP net income (+22% against +18%) because the share count fell: Visa
returned $5.2B through buybacks and dividends in the quarter and $25.6B over the year.

## Balance sheet and returns

$22.1B of debt against $12.9B of cash, so net debt of $9.1B, with $6.9B of free cash flow in the
quarter alone — about three quarters of free cash flow would clear the gross debt. The board
raised the quarterly dividend 16% to **$0.780 per share**, so the ex-dividend date on the quote
page (Aug 11, 2026) and the $2.68 annual rate on the card both predate the raise.
